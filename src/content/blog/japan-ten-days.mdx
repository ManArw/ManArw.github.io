> These ten days in Japan gave me more memories than I could have imagined. This is my attempt to capture them, just the way I felt them, honest and warm, exactly as they happened.

## Day 1 · Touchdown in Tokyo

*Waking up above the clouds felt like stepping into a dream.*

I woke up to a sunrise floating above the clouds, unsure when I had even fallen asleep. The sky outside my window glowed a soft violet, maybe the tint on the glass, maybe just the magic of flying into a new country. Either way, it felt unreal.

Breakfast arrived: a surprisingly good vegetarian meal of pasta, salad, dessert, butter, and a warm bun. Simple, comforting, and exactly what I needed.

### Arrival at Narita Airport

The moment we landed at Narita, we stopped to click a few photos near the welcome poster and then walked towards baggage claim. In perfect timing, our bags slid onto the belt just as we reached it. No waiting, no scrambling, just smooth.

Thanks to the online immigration I’d completed beforehand, customs was quick and hassle-free.

Outside, we waited for my brother, Satwi, who was fashionably late for reasons he absolutely could have avoided. Even in the crowd, I spotted him instantly. He somehow didn’t notice me – shocking, because I'm really not someone you can miss.

I surprised him.

A hug.

A moment of warmth after months apart.

Watching him reunite with our parents was even better, a long, tight embrace that said everything without words.

### First Steps in Tokyo

A little later, Appa, Simba, and I picked up our Welcome Suica cards. From the airport, we took a bus to Tokyo Station, then hopped into a premium taxi straight to Satwi’s apartment.

Once we reached, we relaxed, made tea, sat together, and talked for what felt like hours. Eventually, we walked down the road to check our parents into their hotel.

Then all four of us walked back to the apartment, where Amma cooked uppit for dinner. It had been ages since all of us shared a homemade meal together. That one plate tasted like home.

### Night Out in Akihabara

After dropping our parents back at the hotel, Simba and I decided to explore a bit. A quick train ride later, we were standing in Akihabara, Tokyo’s iconic gaming district.

We spent an hour or two in arcades, losing track of time completely.

The streets outside were alive with neon lights, luxury cars, superbikes, and that electric Akiba energy. We clicked a bunch of photos before stopping at a 7/11 (a konbini) for Starbucks cans and Snickers.

We took the train back to the station near my brother’s apartment and walked home through the calm night streets.

Set up my bed.

Collapsed into sleep.

## Day 2 · The City of Gardens and Skies

The second morning in Tokyo felt softer, calmer, and somehow even more special.

We woke up around 7 AM after one of the most peaceful sleeps ever. The weather outside was cold and gentle, and the mattress felt like it was specifically designed to erase fatigue. After a warm shower, we called Amma and Appa. Amma was still asleep, understandable after the previous day’s exhaustion, so we decided to let her rest a little longer.

They reached Simba’s apartment around 9:45 AM. Amma cooked poha for breakfast, and it tasted incredible. Maybe it was the ingredients here, or maybe just the joy of all of us eating together again.

### Tokyo Metro Adventures

After breakfast, we walked to the nearest metro station and took a train to one of Japan’s main metro hubs, and that place was massive. Absolutely gigantic. From there, we switched lines, skipping one train because it was completely packed. The next one was better, and once we got seats, we relaxed until our stop near the Imperial Palace.

The moment we stepped outside, our minds were blown.

Crystal-clean streets.

Polite pedestrians.

Perfectly disciplined traffic.

Tokyo felt unreal, like a city designed with intention and kindness.

We took plenty of photos and walked along the moat before entering the Imperial Palace East Gardens. The landscape felt like a blend of history and serenity. Every corner carried a story, every path felt preserved with care. We spent a few hours wandering, snacking on the treats we’d brought from India, before deciding our next destination: Tokyo Skytree.

### A Meal Beneath the Skytree

A quick 15-minute metro ride later, we arrived, at Basement Level 2 of the station. We went looking for the Indian restaurant AMARA but, of course, exited from the wrong side. After realizing our mistake, we looped back through the station and found the huge mall beside the Tokyo Skytree.

Lunch was a comforting Indian meal: roti, palak paneer, veg kurma, and a samosa. The food was decent, familiar, and filling, exactly what we needed before heading up the tallest tower in Japan.

### Tokyo Skytree, Touching the Sunset

The crowd inside the Skytree was insane. It took almost an hour to reach the 350-meter observation deck. But the moment we stepped out of the elevator, everything felt worth it.

We caught the last few moments of sunset, the sky glowing warm and red.

Another lift took us up to 450 meters, where the sky had grown darker but the view became even more unreal.

From there, we could still spot Mount Fuji, gently outlined against a red horizon. Planes soared by every few minutes. The city stretched endlessly beneath us, glowing, alive, mesmerizing. We stayed for nearly an hour, clicking photos and simply absorbing the view. I bought a small keepsake: a Tokyo Skytree coin on a chain, something to remind me of that height, that moment.

Returning to the 350-meter deck was chaos again. It took almost thirty minutes to get down, but once we finally made it outside, we sat at one of the tables, ate some Indian snacks, and laughed about the day.

### A Cozy Night Back Home

We took the metro back, switching lines mid-way to get closer to the apartment. Once home, Amma made pulav for dinner while Appa chopped vegetables. Simba and I planned the next day - Fuji-san was calling, and I could feel it.

After dinner, we all stepped out together. Amma and Appa walked back to their hotel room, and Simba and I visited a nearby grocery store to pick up essentials for tomorrow’s adventure.

It had been a long day, a beautiful day, the kind that fills your phone gallery and your heart at the same time.

## Day 3 · Meeting Mount Fuji

Some days feel like dreams. This one felt like a blessing.

We woke up at 5 AM, still sleepy but excited. Today was the day, we were finally going to Kawaguchiko, to see Mt. Fuji. Simba had booked a highway bus, and Amma and Appa arrived at his apartment around 5:30 AM. We packed a simple breakfast of bread and jam, grabbed our jackets, and headed out.

A quiet taxi ride took us to Tokyo Station. Everything was so organized, even at dawn. We boarded the bus, settled into our seats, and almost immediately drifted into sleep.

We had no idea what was waiting for us.

### First Glimpse of the Giant

I woke up to the sound of gasps from a teenager behind me. Curious, I opened my eyes.

And there it was.

Mount Fuji.

Majestic. Massive. Completely unreal.

I froze. For a moment, I forgot to breathe. The perfect snow-capped cone rose against the sky, and I couldn’t take my eyes off it. I fumbled for my camera and snapped a few pictures as we crossed a bridge, where autumn-colored trees framed the entire view like a painting.

<Figure src={fujiClouds} alt="The snow-capped summit of Mount Fuji rising above a band of cloud under a pale blue sky." caption="Fuji-san, floating on its own weather." />

It didn’t feel real, but it was.

### Kawaguchiko: Cold Winds and Hot Coffee

We reached Kawaguchiko Station soon after. The cold wind hit instantly, 12°C, sharp and unforgiving. That alone should’ve told us we weren’t dressed warm enough.

We found a spot to sit, opened our bread and strawberry jam, and ate quietly while our hands froze. Everyone needed coffee, desperately. We located the nearest 7/11, grabbed two hot coffees and some sandwiches, and warmed up just enough to keep going.

### The Ropeway & A Rainbow by the Lake

We began walking through the village toward the Mt. Fuji Ropeway. Light drizzle followed us as we made our way along narrow streets. Then, out of nowhere, Lake Kawaguchiko opened up before us, calm, wide, and shimmering.

And above it, a rainbow.

A full, perfect arc, waiting for us.

I quickly set up my camera and captured all of us with that surreal backdrop.

The ropeway queue was long, and Amma and Appa decided to skip it and rest. Me and Simba waited for nearly an hour before finally getting in. By luck, we got the best seat in the cable car.

At the top, our excitement faded a bit, Fuji had disappeared behind heavy clouds. Not a glimpse of the mountain. We just stood there laughing at our luck, bought some souvenirs, and came back down.

We joined Amma and Appa for lunch as it began to drizzle again. Hot tea became our temporary savior.

### Chasing Fuji Through the Village

We hopped onto a tourist bus that took us across the village to a lakeside garden. And this, this was the view we had come for.

Fuji stood clear, flawless, framed by the lake and gardens.

No clouds.

No haze.

Just the mountain in all its glory.

<Video src="/media/fuji-first-glimpse.mp4" poster="/media/fuji-first-glimpse-poster.jpg" label="A slow pan across Mount Fuji over Lake Kawaguchiko, with pampas grass in the foreground." caption="Lake Kawaguchiko, 3 November. No clouds, no haze." />

We took some incredible photos and soaked in the scenery. The cold, however, was getting harsher as sunset approached. We found a small shop selling hot, sweet potatoes and stood huddled together, letting the warmth slowly return to our fingers.

We then walked toward another viewpoint, the Music Forest Museum, run by the French Republic, but didn’t go inside.

Instead, we ended up on a small bridge just before sunset. The fall colors around us glowed orange and red, and Fuji stood tall in the distance.

I stood there for five minutes in silence.

Just breathing.

Just looking.

Just feeling.

It’s rare to be in the presence of something that makes you feel small in the best way possible.

<Figure wide src={fujiDusk} alt="Mount Fuji at dusk, its snowy peak lit pink by the last sun, above a dark lake and a lit-up bridge." caption="The last light on the summit, from the lake at Kawaguchiko." />

### The Struggle to Get Back

As the cold intensified, we walked to the nearest bus stop. One bus came, completely full. No standing space. Then another. Same situation. A third one… still packed.

The frequency was low and Amma was shivering badly. Fear slowly crept in.

I tried booking a taxi, but none were available. We went into a konbini and asked the staff for help. A kind lady gave us a taxi number, but the driver was already booked.

Minutes felt longer when you’re cold and worried.

Finally, after nearly forty minutes, a bus arrived with just enough space for us to squeeze in. We knew it was probably the last bus of the day, and relief flooded through all of us the moment we stepped inside.

We reached the station on time and boarded the highway bus back to Tokyo. Simba had booked tickets to Shibuya, hoping we’d explore the famous crossing, but we were exhausted beyond measure. No one had the energy to even lift their phones.

We took the metro home. Amma and Appa went straight to their hotel. Me and Simba walked back to his apartment.

Tired, frozen, but hearts full. That night, sleep came instantly.

## Day 4 · A Slow Day in the City

Some days are meant for resting, resetting, and getting ready for what comes next.

We woke up late, the previous day had drained every bit of energy out of us. Mt. Fuji was unforgettable, but our bodies paid the price. Even after a long sleep, we still felt the tiredness in our bones.

### A Warm Shopping Day

By late morning, we stepped out to Uniqlo. The cold in Kawaguchiko had taught us a lesson, so we bought a few jackets to finally stay warm. After that, we walked to Daiso, a supermarket chain filled with everything you could imagine.

We bought a lot of items, small essentials and a few fun things, and I picked up a new tripod. The one I brought from India was unreliable and I was scared my phone would fall. This new one felt sturdy and perfect.

After shopping, we had Indian food for lunch. The conversations flowed so easily that we lost track of time. By the time we finished eating, it was too late to visit the beach for sunset.

Me and Amma sat outside for a while, enjoying the evening. I opened my new tripod to check it out and it was amazing, smooth, steady, exactly what I needed. I packed it back without thinking much.

Later, at home, I wanted to show it to Appa and Simba. That is when I realized the phone holder was missing. I checked everything again and again before it hit me, it must have fallen into the bushes near where we were sitting.

### My Solo Adventure

It was almost 9 PM, but I could not rest without trying. So I took the metro alone, went back to the spot, and searched carefully through the bushes. After a few minutes, there it was, hidden but safe. I felt strangely proud, traveling through a foreign city alone at night, figuring things out by myself. It felt good.

### Ending the Day With Excitement

We had dinner back home, simple and peaceful. I went to bed with a different kind of excitement, because tomorrow we were going to Kyoto, on the Shinkansen, the bullet train.

A childhood dream was finally happening.

## Day 5 · The Day of the Shinkansen

Some dreams stay with you for years, and some days finally bring them to life.

We woke up early to catch the Shinkansen. The kid inside me was already wide awake. Riding a bullet train had been a dream ever since I first read about Japan, and today that dream was finally going to happen.

Japan’s Shinkansen network is unbelievable, a train every five minutes, perfectly on time, perfectly coordinated.

Simba had linked the tickets to our Suica cards, which made the entire check in process at the station almost effortless. We boarded the train and it was spotless, spacious, quiet and incredibly comfortable.

I had asked Satwi to book seats D and E, because that is the side where Mount Fuji becomes visible during the journey.

<Figure src={fujiTrain} alt="Mount Fuji wrapped in cloud behind green hills and a viaduct, seen from a moving train above Japanese houses." caption="Seats D and E. It worked, just about." />

### The Quiet Ride and a Small Realization

The journey was very quiet, almost too quiet. Midway through, me and Satwi were planning what to do first when we reached Kyoto, but then we remembered that the check in time was 3 PM. That seems to be the standard everywhere in Japan.

Our hotel was about four hundred meters from Kyoto Station. We walked there, asked them to store our luggage, and stepped back out to find breakfast.

### Arashiyama, A City Wrapped in Calm

We took the metro to the Arashiyama Bamboo Forest. The city looked beautiful, slow and peaceful. From the station, we decided to walk the one kilometer to the forest. On the way, we passed what looked like a small worship space, there were many stones stacked on each other by visitors. Maybe it was a ritual. I made a small stack myself and said a quiet prayer for my family.

<Figure src={cairn} alt="A small stack of four stones balanced on a rock, with other stacked stones blurred behind." caption="My small stack, on the way to Arashiyama." />

We soon entered the bamboo forest. Many foreign tourists would call it crowded, but for us Indians, it felt normal, just another weekend in a busy market. Living in the most populated country does that to you, nothing feels crowded anymore.

The forest was beautiful and quite large. There were several temples along the way. We bought tickets to a national heritage site and the moment we walked in, we knew it was worth it. The place was mesmerizing. The gardens, the architecture, the calm, everything felt like stepping into a painting.

We were there for almost two hours. Me and Simba decided to explore a different section of the garden, which required some climbing. Amma and Appa stayed back. The plan was to meet at the exit. We did not know there were two exits.

### Losing Them, Finding Them

After ten minutes of walking, me and Simba exited the gardens and waited. Twenty minutes passed and they still had not appeared. Their pocket Wi-Fi had died. There was no way to contact them. Slowly, worry set in.

We asked people around if they had seen an Indian couple leaving the exit. No one had.

We informed the security at the gate and I went back inside to search. I walked the entire garden twice and still could not find them. Then something caught my eye, a board pointing toward another temple. It was an entry we had not taken earlier.

I decided to check it out.

It led to another temple and beside it, another exit. As I walked toward it, my phone buzzed. A message from Amma on WhatsApp. She had managed to find a free Wi-Fi network and sent their location.

I looked around and suddenly saw them in the distance, sitting on a bench. A wave of relief hit me instantly. I called Simba, told him I found them, and asked him to inform the security staff so that they would not worry any further.

I walked up to Amma and Appa. They explained how Amma connected to a free Wi-Fi and quickly sent the message. Somehow everything worked out at the perfect time.

Me and Simba met midway and all of us walked toward the metro station. Hunger had kicked in. We were lucky to find a shop selling vegetarian noodles. We ate and then bought matcha ice cream before heading back to the station.

### Evening in Kyoto

We checked into our hotel and fell asleep almost immediately. The day had drained every bit of energy from us.

We woke up again at around 8 PM and headed to Bic Camera and Don Quijote. After some shopping, we noticed a ramen place that served vegetarian ramen. We went in and tried it. It was delicious. I also had a dessert made from apples.

Satisfied and tired, we returned to our hotel and slept.

## Day 6 · Kyoto in Color and Light

*Some days feel slow, gentle, and almost dreamlike, and Kyoto has a way of making that happen naturally.*

We woke up pretty late, the exhaustion from yesterday’s long adventure still lingering in our legs. Breakfast was simple, some jeera rice and vegetarian biryani, warm and comforting enough to start the day.

### Fushimi Inari, A Thousand Gates and a Thousand Stories

After breakfast, we headed to Fushimi Inari. The moment we reached, the place felt alive with history. The bright orange torii gates stretched endlessly, creating tunnels of color and quiet.

We followed one of the Japanese traditions, praying and lifting a stone to test strength and intention. It was small, but meaningful. We took plenty of pictures, capturing the gates, the paths, and the serene atmosphere.

The hike to the peak had almost ten thousand steps. Amma and Appa decided to stay back. Me and Simba wanted to climb at least halfway, but we knew it would drain our energy completely. Instead, we explored a calm area nearby, with a small lake and a peaceful prayer spot. It felt like a hidden corner of Kyoto, almost untouched.

We bought a few souvenirs and walked back to meet Amma and Appa.

On the way back, we found vegetarian noodles, something surprisingly rare. We ate quickly and then made our way to an Indian restaurant nearby for a proper meal.

### TeamLab Kyoto, A World Made of Light

We had reservations at TeamLab Kyoto. It is a digital art museum and something I had wanted to see for a long time. Satwi had visited the Tokyo one earlier and told me how amazing it was.

He was right.

The moment we entered, everything around us shifted. Lights, sounds, reflections, colors, every room felt like stepping into another dimension. Our slot was at 4 PM and when we finally came out, it was already 7 at night. None of us realized how fast time passed. It felt like we had become children again, wandering through a world built from imagination.

There were activities for all age groups. Some rooms made you think, some rooms made you laugh, some rooms just made you stop and breathe.

By the time we stepped outside, the hunger hit us suddenly. We returned to the hotel and ate, talking about our favorite moments from the museum.

### Ending the Night on a Quiet Note

Tomorrow we had the Shinkansen at around noon. Me and Simba talked for a long time that night, simple conversations that always feel good when you are far from home.

Eventually, we slept, tired but content.

## Day 7 · The Last Morning in Kyoto

*Kyoto looks different in the morning, softer, calmer, almost like it knows you are about to leave.*

We started our day by heading to Kiyomizudera. The walk leading up to the temple felt like stepping into a postcard, narrow streets, traditional houses, shopkeepers arranging displays, everything glowing under the morning light. The crowd slowly grew thicker but there was a charm to it, a kind of beautiful chaos.

As we climbed further, we passed what looked like a small castle structure and a few pagodas rising above the trees. Kiyomizudera itself was peaceful, grand and full of character. We spent some time exploring, taking in the views, and enjoying the atmosphere.

### Souvenirs and a Quick Return

After roaming around for a while, we checked the time. We had a train to catch. On the way down, we bought a few souvenirs for everyone back home, small reminders of Kyoto and its quiet beauty.

Soon after, we made our way to the station and boarded the Shinkansen back to Tokyo. We took seats D and E again, hoping to see Mount Fuji one more time during the journey.

### Back to Tokyo and a Warm Gathering

We reached Tokyo and headed straight to Satwi’s apartment to drop off our luggage. After freshening up a bit, we went to visit family friends. It was a gathering at the house of Ashwini akka, the daughter of Appa’s first colleague. There was warmth, laughter, and that familiar comfort you only feel around people who have known your family for years.

By the time we left, the temperature had dropped. The cold wind hit us hard as we walked back. Once we reached home, we settled in, tired from the travel and the day’s walking.

Eventually, sleep came easily.

## Day 8 · The Last Full Day in Japan

*You never realize how fast time moves until the last day arrives.*

Today was our final full day in Japan. Eight days had passed in a blink. Every morning had felt new, every place had felt exciting, and now the trip was almost ending.

We woke up and made our way to Ginza, one of the most famous luxury streets in Japan. The moment we reached, the atmosphere shifted, tall glass buildings, designer stores, polished roads, and that unmistakable feeling of wealth everywhere. And then came the supercars. A McLaren gliding past, a bright Lamborghini roaring through traffic, and then the king of Japanese performance, the Nissan GT-R, the Godzilla of cars. Seeing it on the streets of Tokyo felt unreal.

We then went into Uniqlo, where I bought a nice jacket for myself, something simple and clean, a memory I could wear.

### A Cruise Through Tokyo

From Ginza, we headed toward the place where the Tokyo city cruise started. The weather was calm, the water reflected the skyline, and the entire experience felt peaceful. I took a time lapse of the cruise, watching the buildings shift and glide past like scenes from a movie.

Later, we reached Odaiba and spent time at the beach side viewpoint. The sunset painted the sky in warm colors but the wind was brutal, cold air sweeping across the water and hitting our faces. Even then, the place felt beautiful, a quiet farewell from Tokyo.

### The Final Walk Home

And just like that, our Japan trip was almost over.

We made our way back home, tired but full of memories, and slept soon after, holding onto the last night in a country that had given us so much in such little time.

## Day 9 · Rain, Goodbyes, and the Last Morning in Japan

*The last day always feels different, a little slower, a little heavier, a little unreal.*

We woke up to the sound of rain. Somehow it felt fitting. The weather had held up the entire trip, clear skies when we needed them the most, and now on the final morning, Tokyo decided to cry a little for us. The temperature had dropped, the air felt colder, and we turned on the heater in Satwi’s apartment.

Amma and Appa had already checked out of their hotel. On their way over, they bought a ton of vegetables for Simba, typical parents who cannot leave without stocking up their child’s kitchen.

We had breakfast together, slow and peaceful. Later, Amma cooked spinach curry and dal so that Satwi would have food for the week. We ate dal and rice for lunch, simple home food on a cold Japanese morning.

### The Ride to Tokyo Station

After packing up, we booked a premium taxi to Tokyo Station. That was where our bus to Narita Airport would arrive. We bought the tickets and waited. The station felt busier than usual, or maybe we were just more aware, holding on to every last moment.

Emotions slowly started creeping in. This trip meant something to each of us. It was a generational journey, a rare one, a trip that may not happen again in the same way.

Soon, it was time to say goodbye to my brother. It felt heavier than I expected. We hugged, talked for a moment, and then time pushed us forward. We boarded the bus to Narita. He walked back toward his apartment.

The city blurred past the bus windows as we left Tokyo, the same city that welcomed us with lights, buildings, trains, warmth, and unforgettable days.

### The Last Few Hours

At Narita Airport, things moved quickly. Check in, immigration, waiting at the gate. None of us spoke much, partly tired and partly holding on to the last bits of Japan before stepping back into routine.

We boarded the plane quietly.

And just like that, ten days flew past us, ten short days filled with a million memories.

## Final note · A Trip That Will Stay With Me Forever

Japan became more than a holiday, it became a feeling. The calm streets of Kyoto, the cold winds of Kawaguchiko, the neon nights of Tokyo, the sound of trains rushing past, the warmth of family meals, the laughter during small moments, the mountains, the sunsets, the vending machines, the noodles, the kindness of strangers, and the simplicity of everyday life there.

Some trips change you in small ways, quietly, without announcing themselves. This was one of those trips.

We came home with heavier suitcases and fuller hearts, and as the plane lifted off, it felt like Japan was gently saying, come back when you can, I will be waiting.

> In the end, it wasn’t Japan that made the trip special, it was the people I saw it with.
